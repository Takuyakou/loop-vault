# VL-12 — Interaction, audition, BPM, Transport, and Next Move

## Scope and findings

VL-12a removes keyboard focus from the noninteractive Current and Next outer surfaces. Those surfaces had `tabIndex=0`, so Chromium could draw its standard outline while the document shortcut handled Space. The inner scroll regions remain keyboard focusable with the teal application focus ring. Text inputs and dialogs retain their own keys. A focused button owns Space through its native click; Arrow, Home/End, F, M, R and Escape remain document shortcuts outside editing controls. The VL-09 geometry test now focuses the actual scroll region rather than the removed outer tab stop.

VL-12b audited the card audition path. `Tone.start()` resumes the shared context and does not create a new AudioContext on each click. The old path reused one instrument for repeated clicks, but released its old voices and scheduled a new attack only 12 ms later on the same instrument. Piano and Electric Piano have long release tails, so rapid clicks could overlap. Seek/stop disposed the audition instrument immediately after release, which could truncate a nonzero waveform. The new path reuses two bounded instrument/output banks per active timbre. The outgoing bank ramps its Gain to zero over 12 ms; the incoming attack starts after 5 ms and ramps up over 12 ms. A retired bank is disposed after 24 ms, with at most two retired banks retained during rapid stop/seek bursts. Generation checks reject stale async loads. No debounce, limiter, MIDI output change, or AudioContext recreation was added. Instrument creation occurs on a bank's first use or a timbre change, rather than every card click. Final click/pop quality remains a human listening check.

VL-12c makes the entire BPM field a pointer-captured vertical scrub target. Movement below 3 px remains a click into editable text; normal drag is 4 px/BPM, Shift is 10 px/BPM, and Ctrl applies a fivefold coarse multiplier. Click and double click select the editable value. Wheel changes 1 BPM; Arrow changes 1 BPM or 10 with Shift. All paths round and clamp to the existing 30–240 range, invoke the existing `changeBpm` / transport tempo path only when the integer changes, and leave source tempo provenance untouched. The text input keeps spinbutton value semantics and allows full selection. Scrub works during playback without changing the scheduler design.

VL-12d keeps the Transport at 92 px. The two rows have constrained grid tracks, a smaller vertical inset, and explicit hidden vertical overflow while preserving horizontal fallback. The second row's top padding was reduced so its controls fit. An inset teal focus outline remains visible inside each clipped row. In browser measurements, row 1 was 41/41 px (client/scroll); row 2 was 40/40 px or 41/41 px depending on scaling. Measurements covered 1920×1080, 1440×900, 1280×800, 1280×720, and effective 125%, 150%, and 200% scaling.

VL-12e retains ten fixed fingers and the 68 px strip. Left and right are separate, symmetric five-slot DOM groups with each hand's summary centered above its own slots. KEEP shows a moderate hand-color border, a four-pixel continuation band, the finger ID and pitch, and an accessible “押さえたまま” description. An unused finger shows only its ID with a dashed low-emphasis border. The ID keeps readable contrast. Moving, ADD, and RELEASE states remain stronger than KEEP; LEFT amber and RIGHT cyan semantics remain. The grouping was checked at 1920 width, 1280 width, and 150% scaling. A public synthetic component test verifies a present KEEP state and empty state; the browser fixture does not contain a KEEP state on its sampled cards.

## Verification

The stage has focused unit tests for the audio voice lifecycle (including ten rapid alternating clicks and cleanup), BPM/space interactions, and synthetic KEEP/empty presentation. Browser tests cover native-control Space ownership, direct BPM input, left/handle scrubbing, modifier sensitivity, wheel, keyboard, clamp, pointer capture outside the field, seven Transport geometry conditions, and hand-group placement. Existing VL-09–11, P8.1/P8.2, Vault, MIDI tempo, and Voicing Loop regressions remain in the candidate and post-merge gates.

The first candidate browser pass found two VL-12 regressions: filtering every key on buttons disabled established Arrow/Home/End navigation, and applying opacity to the entire empty slot lowered finger-ID contrast. Both were isolated and corrected. The VL-09 geometry test's old focus target became intentionally nonfocusable; it now targets the retained inner scroll region. The subsequent focused browser and unit runs passed. Full candidate and post-merge gate totals, merge commit, and direct EXE metadata are recorded in the task closeout after the final HEAD runs.

No Analyzer, Extractor, Identity/Decoder, Vault schema, source note/timing, or Phase 9 behavior was changed. No private media or Vault data is included.
